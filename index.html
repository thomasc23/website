Thomas Chamberlain

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Political Science at the University of Rochester, where I use quantitative methods to study American politics with a focus on elections and voting behavior. In my dissertation, I study how changing the issue agenda affects the trade-offs voters are willing to make between policy congruence on different issue dimensions and how these new trade-offs change the kinds of policy platforms that can win majority support. I am also broadly interested in measurement——e.g., of voter ideology in online surveys, of racially polarized voting in strategic redistricting environments——and American political development. Methodologically, my work often involves designing and fielding survey experiments, estimating various models of voting, and computer simulation studies.

Previously, I received a B.A. in political philosophy from Stanford University and worked in NYC politics.
